\section{Conclusion}

The Sanguine case shows how an SDM approach changes the design of clinical AI. The project did not stop at model selection. It linked stakeholder needs, architecture decisions, uncertainty, development planning, and risk mitigation into one managed system.

The main conclusion is straightforward: for clinical AI, the safest architecture may not be the cheapest or fastest one, and the most capable model may not be the dominant design choice. The stronger baseline is the one that makes clinical accuracy, traceability, regulatory readiness, workflow fit, and institutional trust explicit.

The next version of this paper should add figures from the tradespace and DSM work, tighten the citations, and decide whether the article is positioned for systems engineering, engineering management, health AI governance, or an arXiv preprint across those categories.
